Trading Instructions & Notes

Comprehensive operational guidelines, risk boundaries, and execution methodologies for market participation.

01. Risk Management & Capital Preservation

Never enter any trade without defining your risk parameters beforehand. Capital preservation is the absolute priority for long-term survival, stability, and consistency in financial markets.

  • Position Sizing: Always calculate your exact position size relative to your total account portfolio value to cushion against unexpected drawdowns.
  • Risk Percentage: Never risk more than 1% to 2% of your overall trading capital on a single setup or transaction.
  • Stop-Loss Protocol: Always use predefined stop-loss orders immediately upon entry to completely eliminate emotional decision-making when the market moves against you.

Detailed Note: Accounts that fail to maintain rigid stop-loss practices face structural failure during high-volatility sessions. Treat capital as your inventory.

02. Market Analysis, Setup & Timing

Patience is paramount. Wait exclusively for high-probability setups that align directly with your overarching market bias rather than forcing trades out of boredom, frustration, or FOMO.

  • Multi-Timeframe Confluence: Perform thorough macro-to-micro timeframe analysis before committing capital to any position.
  • Structural Zones: Pinpoint critical historical support, resistance, liquidity pools, and trend structures before executing trades.
  • High-Impact Events: Avoid opening fresh intraday positions during major scheduled news releases or central bank rate announcements unless managing existing swings.

Detailed Note: Missing a move is infinitely better than entering a bad setup. Protect your psychological bandwidth by staying sidelined during choppy consolidation periods.

03. Psychological Discipline & Execution

Execution must be completely mechanical and detached from emotional highs and lows. Adhere strictly to your pre-market checklist without deviation or improvised exceptions.

  • Comprehensive Journaling: Log every single trade with its precise entry, exit, mathematical reasoning, setup grade, and your emotional state.
  • Accepting Loss: View consecutive or single losses as a normal, budgeted cost of doing business in volatile financial ecosystems.
  • Daily Circuit Breakers: Immediately step away from the trading screens the moment you hit your maximum daily loss threshold or target profit goal.

Detailed Note: Revenge trading after a loss is the primary cause of account liquidation. Honor your daily limits and walk away.
