01 / The Process

A structured trading workflow, step by step.

Trading involves more than pressing buy or sell. A consistent workflow helps you make decisions using defined rules instead of urgency or emotion. Practise with a simulator before considering real money.

⌕

1. Prepare the Market View

Identify the instrument, trend, important price levels, current volatility and relevant scheduled news.

  • Use reliable market information.
  • Mark important levels beforehand.
  • Know when you should avoid trading.
⌁

2. Define a Valid Setup

Write down the conditions required before considering a trade. Your rules should be clear enough to review later.

  • Define the entry trigger.
  • Identify conditions that cancel the idea.
  • Avoid entering from fear of missing out.
⊙

3. Plan Entry and Exit

Identify the intended entry price, stop-loss or exit rule, target logic and the maximum intended risk.

  • Define your exit before entering.
  • Account for fees and slippage.
  • Remember that stop orders cannot guarantee a fill price.
▦

4. Calculate Position Size

Position size connects account equity and planned risk with the distance between entry and stop.

  • Choose the risk amount first.
  • Check the product's lot size.
  • Never size a trade based on excitement alone.
◷

5. Manage the Open Trade

Follow your written rules instead of reacting impulsively to every price movement.

  • Respect your exit conditions.
  • Avoid uncontrolled averaging down.
  • Follow your daily loss limit.
✎

6. Journal and Review

Record the reason for entry, risk, execution, outcome and lessons after every trade.

  • Record trading costs.
  • Separate process quality from outcome.
  • Review a meaningful sample of trades.
02 / Protect Your Capital

Risk management is the foundation.

No trading method eliminates risk. Clear risk controls can help you avoid exposing too much capital to a single decision.

Rules to Establish Before Trading

  • ✓ Risk budget: Choose an amount you can afford to lose. No percentage is universally safe for every trader.
  • ✓ Daily loss limit: Decide in advance when you will stop trading for the day.
  • ✓ Combined exposure: Consider the risk across correlated positions.
  • ✓ Trading costs: Include applicable brokerage, taxes, spreads and slippage.
  • ✓ Leverage: Understand that leverage magnifies losses as well as gains.

Understand Reward-to-Risk

Reward-to-risk compares the planned reward with the planned loss if the stop is reached. It does not predict the outcome.

Planned Risk Potential Reward Reward:Risk
₹500 ₹500 1:1
₹500 ₹1,000 2:1
₹500 ₹1,500 3:1
Important: A higher reward-to-risk ratio does not automatically make a strategy profitable. Win rate, costs, execution and market conditions also matter.
03 / Understand the Numbers

Position sizing explained.

Position sizing estimates how many units fit your planned risk. The calculator below uses a simplified spot-style model and does not automatically account for every derivative contract, lot size, leverage or margin requirement.

Term Meaning Why It Matters
Account equity Capital used for the calculation. Sets the base for the risk amount.
Risk percentage Share of equity allocated to planned loss on one trade. Defines the intended risk budget.
Entry price Price at which you plan to enter. Used to estimate position value.
Stop-loss price Level where the trade plan is invalidated or exited. Determines the entry-to-stop distance.
Quantity Approximate units that fit the risk budget. Must also fit funds and product specifications.

05 / Trading Discipline

Common mistakes to recognise early.

Many avoidable errors begin with decisions made outside the plan. Clear rules can help you pause before acting.

01

Revenge Trading

Trying to recover a loss immediately. Take a break and respect your trading limits instead.

02

Fear of Missing Out

Entering because the price has already moved. Wait for your next valid setup.

03

Moving the Stop Impulsively

Changing your risk boundary to avoid accepting a loss can expose you to much larger losses.

04

Overtrading

More trades do not necessarily mean more opportunity. Focus on setups that meet your rules.

05

Ignoring Trading Costs

Brokerage, spreads, taxes and slippage can affect your net result, especially with frequent trading.

06

Judging Too Quickly

A few wins or losses do not prove whether a strategy works. Review a consistent sample.

06 / Continuous Improvement

Build a simple trading journal.

Keep records so your reviews are based on evidence rather than memory. A notebook or spreadsheet can work.

▧

Before the Trade

Record the date, instrument, setup, entry trigger, stop, target logic, planned risk and reason for entry.

↔

During the Trade

Record rule-based adjustments, execution issues, major news and whether you followed the plan.

✓

After the Trade

Record the net result, costs, rule adherence, one lesson and one improvement to test.

07 / Frequently Asked Questions

Trading guide FAQs.

Understand the fundamentals, practise without risking capital and seek qualified advice when needed.

Can beginners use this trading guide?
Yes. It explains a structured workflow and basic position-sizing concepts. It does not teach a guaranteed strategy or replace supervised practice or professional advice.
Does the calculator tell me which trade to take?
No. It estimates position size using your inputs. It cannot assess whether a setup is valid, predict prices or determine whether a trade is suitable for your circumstances.
Is risking 1% per trade always safe?
No percentage is universally safe. Risk tolerance depends on your financial circumstances, experience, strategy and market. Gaps, slippage and leverage can cause losses beyond the simplified estimate.
Can I use this calculator for futures, options or leveraged crypto?
Do not rely on this simplified model for those products without adapting it for contract multipliers, lot sizes, premiums, funding, margin, liquidation mechanics and other product risks.
What should I do after consecutive losses?
Follow your predefined daily or weekly stop rule. Pause, review whether you followed your plan and avoid increasing position size to recover losses.
How do I know whether a strategy works?
Define the rules, test them consistently on suitable historical data, include realistic costs and slippage, and validate them through controlled forward testing. Past performance does not guarantee future results.
