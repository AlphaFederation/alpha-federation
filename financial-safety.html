1. Spot Investment Scams

Key concept

Scams may promise guaranteed high returns, urgent limited-time opportunities or secret strategies. Fraudsters may use fake testimonials, impersonate well-known brands or show fabricated profits.

Example: A stranger promising to double money in days is a warning sign, even if the website looks professional or a friend shared it.

Practical checklist

Verify registration and details through official regulator or company channels. Never transfer money based only on screenshots, social-media claims or pressure.

Remember: This lesson is for general education. It does not recommend buying or selling any security. Consider your own circumstances and consult a qualified professional where appropriate.

2. Protect OTPs, PINs and Passwords

Key concept

OTP, UPI PIN, card PIN and passwords are private credentials. Genuine support staff should not need you to disclose them. Scammers may pose as bank staff, delivery agents or customer care.

Example: If a caller asks you to install a remote-access app and share an OTP to stop an account block, end the call and contact the institution independently.

Practical checklist

Use unique passwords, enable available multi-factor authentication, secure your SIM and device, and never share remote access.

Remember: This lesson is for general education. It does not recommend buying or selling any security. Consider your own circumstances and consult a qualified professional where appropriate.

3. Fake Links and Impersonation

Key concept

Phishing uses fake websites, messages or emails to steal credentials or money. Lookalike domains and sponsored search results can imitate legitimate services.

Example: A message claiming your KYC expires today may lead to a fake login page. Open the official app or type the known official address yourself instead.

Practical checklist

Check the full domain, avoid unknown attachments and do not trust caller ID alone. Report suspicious messages using official channels.

Remember: This lesson is for general education. It does not recommend buying or selling any security. Consider your own circumstances and consult a qualified professional where appropriate.

4. UPI and QR-Code Safety

Key concept

A QR code can initiate a payment. Scanning a code or approving a collect request should never be done without understanding the transaction. A PIN authorises a payment, not receipt of money.

Example: If a buyer asks you to scan a QR code and enter your PIN to receive sale proceeds, do not proceed. Verify the payment in your own bank app.

Practical checklist

Check recipient name and amount, reject unexpected collect requests and never share your screen during a payment.

Remember: This lesson is for general education. It does not recommend buying or selling any security. Consider your own circumstances and consult a qualified professional where appropriate.

5. Broker and Demat Account Security

Key concept

Use the broker’s official website or app and check account alerts, statements and transaction confirmations. Fraudsters may impersonate brokers or offer help with account activation.

Example: Do not give remote access to a person claiming they can complete KYC or unlock a referral reward. Contact support through the official app.

Practical checklist

Enable security features, review holdings and transactions, protect account recovery details and report suspicious activity quickly.

Remember: This lesson is for general education. It does not recommend buying or selling any security. Consider your own circumstances and consult a qualified professional where appropriate.

6. What to Do After Suspected Fraud

Key concept

Act quickly if money or credentials may be compromised. Contact your bank or payment provider through official channels, block affected access where appropriate and preserve transaction details and communications.

Example: In India, suspected cyber financial fraud can be reported promptly through the national cybercrime helpline 1930 and the official cybercrime reporting portal.

Practical checklist

Do not pay a recovery agent who guarantees funds back. Keep complaint numbers and follow instructions from your bank and authorities.

Remember: This lesson is for general education. It does not recommend buying or selling any security. Consider your own circumstances and consult a qualified professional where appropriate.

7. Verify Financial Products and Advisers

Key concept

Before using a financial service, check the entity, product terms, charges, risks and regulatory status using official sources. Registration or a professional-looking website does not make every claim safe.

Example: Compare the product name and provider details on the official regulator or provider website instead of trusting an advertisement alone.

Practical checklist

Avoid sharing identity documents with unverified contacts and read privacy and consent notices before submitting information.

Remember: This lesson is for general education. It does not recommend buying or selling any security. Consider your own circumstances and consult a qualified professional where appropriate.

8. Privacy and Identity Protection

Key concept

Personal information such as PAN, Aadhaar details, bank statements and account credentials can be misused if shared carelessly. Provide only what is needed through a verified, secure channel.

Example: If an unknown person asks for a full bank statement and ID documents over chat to release a reward, verify why they need it before sharing anything.

Practical checklist

Use device locks, keep software updated, restrict app permissions and monitor account alerts. Mask documents when a full number is not required.

Remember: This lesson is for general education. It does not recommend buying or selling any security. Consider your own circumstances and consult a qualified professional where appropriate.
